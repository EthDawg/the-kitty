## 2.0, phase B: the row and the review — 4 November 2026 (unreleased, branch `v2-row`; staging artifact only, the public page is untouched)
- **Merchant dictionary** (`merchants/`): 370 Australian merchants, generic transfers, fees, income and government descriptors, each with regex patterns over a normalised descriptor, a category from the sheet's Categories tab, a brand hue, a website and a kind; `categories.json` maps every category to a brand icon and a hue. `Engine.normalise` strips card suffixes, store numbers, dates, amounts, trailing state and capital-city tokens, `PTY LTD` and `AU`; `Engine.merchant` tries the sheet's Rules (tokens in order, tolerant of a descriptor a bank cut at 24 characters), then the dictionary, then a flagged Title Case fallback. Measured over the 925 historical rows: Rules then dictionary resolve all 925; the dictionary alone misses 2.4% (`merchants/README.md`). No logos in the repo: a gitignored `logos/` pack is embedded at build if a household supplies one.
- **EntryRow** (`src/kit/entry-row.js`): one component renders anything that happened: a monogram (two letters on a disc in the brand hue) with the category icon from the brand sprite pinned to it, the clean merchant name, the amount with its sign in tabular numerals, a second line (category, cadence of a matching Contract, account, person, "first time here"), the raw descriptor in the Review card and the drawer, and badges that mean something (pending, recurring, refund, possible double, needs review, split, transfer pair, no mate, worth it). A trade renders as "25 units at $48.20", a valuation as "balance from statement" or "value from an appraisal". `EntryList` groups by day with the day's outgoings. The build replaces the shell's 16 hand-drawn symbols with the 95-icon brand sprite under the same ids, and categories carry brand icons in the jar list, the category drawer and the entry drawer.
- **Activity** rebuilt on `EntryRow` and `EntryList`: search (now over the bank's own words too), people and flow chips, a Balances & trades view on a v2 sheet, month windows (all time, month, 3 months, 12 months, FY, with earlier and later), CSV export with the raw text and kind, 80 rows first and more as you scroll. Measured in headless Chromium over 5,000 bundled entries: Activity renders in 19 ms median (7 runs, 18 to 29 ms) after the model is built.
- **Review queue** at the top of Activity and a tile on Home: uncategorised rows, descriptors no rule knows, the sheet's possible doubles (same merchant, amount, account and day), transfers without a mate, and a first visit above `big_bill_amount` in the as-of month. One or two taps each, keyboard first on a desktop (1 to 3 pick a suggestion, C categorise, Enter confirm, D merge, T match, S split, J and K move). A categorise writes the entry's `category_id` cell on Entries by explicit range plus a Decisions row; confirm, merge a double, match a transfer and split write only a Decisions row (`ref_kind entry, decision, value, by, at`). Decisions overlay the model on read, so the page agrees with the sheet the moment a row is written and never remembers a judgment the sheet does not have; in the demo, and on a sheet that is read-only for the viewer, a judgment stays on screen for the visit and the sheet is never written.
- **Fortune cookie**: a review row no rule knows shows the closed cookie from the brand kit; cracking it asks Claude (`sample.json`) for `{merchant, category_id, confidence, why}` from the raw text, the normalised text, the dictionary's nearest entries and the category list, and the slip shows the proposal with "Make it a rule" and "Not this". "Make it a rule" writes a Rules row by explicit range (`pattern` is the normalised descriptor, `match` normalised, `source` cookie), applies the category to this row and every unreviewed row the pattern matches (cell writes plus Decisions rows) and says how many. One cookie open at a time, never opened on load; under reduced motion the slip shows at once. A rule that only says `UNCAT` does not count as knowing the merchant.
- **Worth it?**: up to five discretionary rows over `worth_it_min` (a Kitty-tab key, default 40, written once if absent) from the most recent week that has any, one tap each (yes, meh, no; Y, M, N on a keyboard), each a Decisions row `worth_it`; Goals shows "Spend that earned a yes" by category and merchant once five answers exist. Copy grounded in Dunn, Gilbert and Wilson (2011): experiences over things, many small pleasures, pay now and consume later. No streaks, no badges, no score.
- **Tests**: `test-v2.js` grows from 14 to 24 (normaliser cases from real descriptors, dictionary coverage and the miss-rate ceiling, the review queue on both fixtures and the live snapshot, the decisions overlay with the transfer invariant unchanged, worth-it selection, EntryRow for every entry kind, 5,000 entries in node). Screenshots in `docs/screenshots/2.0/`.
- **Not done, by design or by limit**: the first `sample` call raises claude.ai's "Use Claude" consent, which only the owner can click, so the cookie's live proposal is verified with a mocked `sample` headlessly, not against Claude; the kit gallery, the signal-driven widgets and the public cut-over are phase C; the loans stay statement-valued (phase A's note stands).
## 2.0, in progress

- Phase C, the canvas: Home is widgets with manifests (`src/kit/widgets.js`), switched on by signals, added once with a toast and an undo, never as empty tiles. GridStack 14.0.0 from cdnjs with its stylesheet vendored and inlined; resize snaps to declared widths and heights follow the content; the page stays a working CSS grid if the script is blocked. Layout is a per-person preference, never in the sheet; `?layout=default` for screenshots. Phone: one column, a drag handle and arrows. Setup explains it in five sentences. `dist/kit.html` is the component gallery, drawn by the page's own functions, in four states in `portals/test/kit.spec.js`; CI diffs against baselines in `docs/screenshots/kit/ci/`. Not done: the cutover to the public artifact.
- Portals, second cut: eight years. `portals/history.py` invents March 2019 to June 2025 for the Davies household and lands every account on the sheet's opening balances to the cent, so the portals hold 4,179 transactions, 417 payslips and about 1,250 documents (monthly statements, end-of-year summaries, income statements, renewals). The portals now read like the sites they parody: an overview with balances, an activity page per account with financial-year presets, search and paging, an Export panel, and a Statements & documents page by financial year. `build-data.py --write-sources` writes the history as 405 bank files under `sources/` (the simulator's months untouched), so an agent can rebuild the sheet from files alone; the Playwright job imports a whole financial year month by month and shows the closing-balance check holding a month out of order. Descriptors before July 2025 vary the way real exports do (store numbers, state and country tokens, truncation, PTY LTD, CommBank's card and value-date suffix); the conventions are in `portals/README.md`.
- Importer: a file that ends by an account's opening date and before the months being imported is history from before the ledger, counted as outside the month. Eight years of portal history under `sources/` no longer stops every run; a file that reaches the month, or a month before the account opened, still stops it.
- Importer, hardened for hammered use (phase "import", branch `v2-import`). Every source row has an identity (account, settled date, amount, description as delivered, occurrence); within a run a row seen in several files counts once at the most copies any one file holds, never the sum, and against the sheet rows are refused by identity and IDs checked never to collide. The importer never writes to the sheet: it emits `write-plan.json` (exact ranges from the next empty row, Entries A–T in chunks of at most 500 rows, Payslips A–G, Contracts updates by cell, preconditions on the last known row) and `period-close/verify.py` compares a saved re-read with the plan cell by cell; the bank-export skill documents the three steps (plan, write by explicit range, verify) and `--dry-run`; the nonexistent `--next-id` is gone. Fail loud: an unknown account folder, a folder that is not a download date, an unrecognised shape, a header mismatch, a PDF with no adapter, a zero-byte file, a month-first date (every shape declares its date order), a day that does not exist, a row dated after the day its file was downloaded or after `--as-of`, a row before the account opened: each stops the run with exit 1 and a `review.md` that names the file, the row and what to do; a byte order mark is accepted and noted; rows outside the month are counted, never skipped silently. New adapters feed Entries: the lender's loan statement (interest charged as a transaction on the loan, the repayment as the loan-side mate of the bank row by `transfer_id`, the closing balance as a month-end valuation; the transactions and the pairing wait under `deferred` until phase D makes the loans ledger accounts, `--loans now` writes them), the property manager's owner statement as a reconciliation source (the rental account's bank rows itemise rent and fees, so every line must match one and a difference holds the run; the clearing-account path for an agent that pays only the net is documented in `adapters.py`, not built), Pawroll payslips de-duplicated by pay date, person and employer, and the insurer's PDFs read as text by a standard-library extractor (`pdftext.py`): a renewal that differs from the Contracts row plans the update, a PDF the reader cannot parse stops with "this PDF needs a person". Transfers between own accounts are paired on import (a mate of the opposite amount within four days, X ids continuing from the sheet). Each run is kept under `period-close/<month>/runs/<timestamp>/`; a range (`2025-07..2027-03`) backfills in one run. Measured: every month the portals serve (21 months, 258 files of every kind) plans 1,085 transactions, 54 valuations and 91 payslips in 0.11 s with no duplicate identity and a repeat that plans nothing; a 100-month simulator backfill of 5,000-odd rows plans in chunks under a second. Tests: `period-close/test_import.py` (standard library, 39 tests) replaces `test_import_month.py` and runs in CI. `period-close/pull_portals.py` downloads every file the portals serve for the scale run.
- Importer: overlapping exports in one run take each row once (a card calendar export plus a statement export gave 38 rows for 25), and a closing balance that disagrees with the ledger holds the run. Tests in `period-close/test_import.py`.
- Portals on Vercel (phase E): https://the-kitty-portals.vercel.app. Seven parody portals with original wordmarks and a DEMO banner (Westpaw, CommBark, Amewx, PING, PropertyMeow, Pawroll, Purrsure) sign in with any name and the password `demo` and return server-generated downloads in the real institutions' column shapes, PDFs through pdf-lib. `portals/build-data.py` builds the data from the sheet's snapshot and the simulator, so a closed month downloaded there is byte-identical to the file under `sources/`. The CommBank headerless shape joins `period-close/adapters.py`; `import_month.py` takes `--sources` and `--out` and reads each account's institution from the sheet's Accounts tab. A Playwright job in CI downloads every shape, imports, reconciles, and shows a repeated month appends nothing. Jordan's old everyday at CommBark exists only in the portals, labelled.

## 2.0, phase A: the ledger — 4 November 2026 (unreleased, branch `v2-ledger`)
- A copy of the demo sheet, "Digital Davies v2" (`kitty.config.json`, `v2_sheet`), migrated to the ledger model. The published 1.1 page and the sheet it reads are untouched; the artifact was not republished.
- New tabs: Accounts extended to every store of value (the six cash accounts, the home, the Geelong townhouse, both loans, two supers, the ETFs, the car) with class, valuation method, currency, status, purpose and linked account; Entries (925 transactions with Up's vocabulary, 152 valuations from appraisals and statements, one trade); Balances (every account at every month end, by formula: anchor plus entries, or the last valuation carried forward); Contracts (17 subscriptions and policies, the two loan terms, the lease); Decisions (keep or cancel per contract, a planned refresh); Rules (85 descriptor patterns from history).
- Transactions, Valuations, Assets and Subscriptions became formula views over the ledger, so every 1.1 formula and the 1.1 page shape keep working. Net Worth derives from Balances and reproduces the 1.1 figures to the dollar. Every existing ID is stable; no row was lost.
- All 152 transfer rows paired by `transfer_id` (76 same-day pairs, no orphans). Two new Compliance Checks: "Transfers without a mate" and "Balance change explained (latest month)", both OK on the Davies data.
- Beta fixtures, marked beta and off on the page: a crypto wallet, an SMSF, a laptop with a planned refresh date, an RSU vest (a trade plus a market valuation).
- Engine: the model builds accounts, entries, balances, contracts, decisions and rules from a v2 snapshot or a connector read, and a 1.1 snapshot still builds; `balanceAt`, `signals`, `transfers`, `invariants`; the trust drawer reconciles every account against the Balances tab and reports each, then the ledger's invariants.
- Tests: `test-v2.js` (14 tests) over the live v2 snapshot and the fixtures `everyday-only` and `everyday+property`; the 13 existing tests still pass; the importer appends nothing on a repeated month against either snapshot. `build.py --v2`, `snapshot.py --v2`, the Apps Script serves the new tabs, `tools/grid_to_snapshot.py` builds the v2 snapshot from connector reads.
- Not done, by design or by limit: the copy's Apps Script feed is not deployed (one click in the sheet, see `snapshot.py`); the loans are statement-valued rather than ledger-built, because the home-loan repayment stays an expense in Cash Flow and splitting principal from interest would change the spend figures the oracle pins; Google Sheets does not broadcast array criteria through SUMIFS, COUNTIFS or MAXIFS, so Balances, the Subscriptions view and the Contracts next date use MAP and LAMBDA.
